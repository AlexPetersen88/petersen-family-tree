Chapter 1 — A Boy Called Hans Pedersen of Skee (1833–1847)

This chapter follows Hans Pedersen (P18), the father of the emigrant Hans Peter Petersen. Everything here comes from Danish church books and censuses. No family memory reaches back this far.

Born at Valsømagle

Hans Pedersen was born on 25 February 1833 at Valsømagle, a village in Haraldsted parish, Ringsted district, Sorø county, on the island of Zealand (S51, S52, S53). He was baptized in Haraldsted church on 31 March 1833, which was Palm Sunday (S51, S52). The church stood only about 2 miles straight-line from Valsømagle, so the walk from the village to the font was a short one (S51, S53).

The parish clerk entered him as uægte, meaning born outside marriage (S51, S52). His mother was an unmarried servant (pige / tjenestepige) from Valsømagle. The church books do not agree on her full name. The hovedministerialbog baptism of 1833 writes her as Ane Marie Clausdatter of Valsømagle (S51). The kontraministerialbog copy of the same baptism carries an interlinear correction that reads Hansd. over a struck word (S52). Fourteen years later, at his confirmation, the book again calls her Ane Marie Hansd. (S56). Move lists around the birth year also use the Hansdatter form: Ane Marie Hansd., age 28, tjenestepige, going to and from Ringsted in 1832–1833 (S59, S60). Ringsted town lies roughly 4 miles straight-line from Valsømagle: the mother's moves and the named father's home both sat within a morning's walk of the child's birthplace (S51, S59, S60). This story keeps both readings in view. Genealogist has not closed the Clausdatter / Hansdatter conflict (P25).

The same baptism names a man as the child’s father. In Danish church books an udlagt barnefader is the man the mother named as father; the entry is not proof of paternity. The 1833 Haraldsted baptism writes Peder Johansen of Ringsted as that named father (S51). The 1847 confirmation repeats the same wording: Uægte Søn af Pigen Ane Marie Hansd. og udlagt Barnefader Peder Johansen — illegitimate son of the girl Ane Marie Hansd. and named child’s father Peder Johansen — with pleiefader (foster father) Huusm. Jens Nielsen of Skee (S56). No later record cleared for this story proves that Peder Johansen was in fact the father, and Genealogist keeps him as a candidate only (P28), not a linked parent. The controversy is part of Hans’s beginning: the books name a father; the evidence does not close the case.

A foster son in Skee

Hans did not grow up with his mother. On the census of 18 February 1834, when he was one year old, he was living in the village of Skee in Haraldsted parish, about 3 miles straight-line from Valsømagle where he was born and about 3 miles from Haraldsted church. He was listed as a pleiebarn (foster child) in the household of Jens Nielsen, a cottager (husmand) and wheelwright, and his wife Kirsten Johansdatter (S54).

He was still there at the next two censuses:

  • 1 February 1840: "Hans Pedersen, 6, unmarried, foster child" in the household of Jens Nielsen, 49, and Kirsten, 44 (S55).
  • 1 February 1845: "Hans Pedersen, 12," born in the parish, now called a pleiesøn (foster son) (S57).

So from his first year until at least age twelve, the cottage of Jens and Kirsten in Skee was his home (S54, S55, S57). No record found so far shows that the foster parents were related to him or to his mother.

The records show that Jens Nielsen worked as a wheelwright (S54). Many years later, Hans himself would be a wheelwright, a hjulmand (S36, S37). The sources do not say who taught him the trade. We can only note that he grew up in a wheelwright's home, and that by 1850 Jens Nielsen was still entered as huusmand and, in a hard-to-read word, perhaps hjulmager (S58). The thread of the trade runs unbroken from the foster father's cottage to Hans's own marriage entry fifteen years later (S36).

Confirmation, 1847

On 11 April 1847, the first Sunday after Easter, Hans was confirmed in Haraldsted church. He was entered as boy no. 3: "Hans Pedersen of Skee," born 25 February 1833 at Valsømagle and vaccinated 29 June 1833 by Aschlund (S56). The full confirmation line is the richest single sentence we have for his childhood: Hans Pedersen af Skee … Uægte Søn af Pigen Ane Marie Hansd. og udlagt Barnefader Peder Johansen — Pleiefader Huusm. Jens Nielsen i Skee (S56). In one entry the parish records his foster home in Skee, his mother’s Hansd. reading, and the still-unproven named father from Ringsted. Folio 41, no. 1026, is the book’s own reference for that confirmation (S56).

What we do not know yet

  • Whether Peder Johansen of Ringsted was truly Hans’s father remains open. The church books name him; Genealogist has not proven the link (S51, S56; P28 candidate only).
  • Which of the mother’s surnames is correct — Clausdatter or Hansdatter — is also open (S51, S52, S56, S59, S60; P25).
  • At the census of 1 February 1850 Hans, then about 17, was no longer in the Skee foster household (S58). Where he was living that year has not been found.
  • The years between 1850 and 1862 are blank in our records. The next time we see him, he is a young man working at Bjernedegaard in Bjernede parish (Chapter 2).
