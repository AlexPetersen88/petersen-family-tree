# Chapter 3 — Hans Peter Crosses to Minnesota (1884–1922)

*This chapter covers Hans Peter Petersen (P6) and Maren Sophia Jensen (P7). It draws on Danish emigration and church records, US censuses, Minnesota death records, and Find a Grave. Find a Grave is a secondary source. Michael Don Petersen has confirmed that Hans Peter and Maren Sophia were Elmer's parents.*

## Leaving through Copenhagen, April 1884

On 16 April 1884, the Copenhagen Police emigration register recorded "Hans Peter Petersen" of Bjernede, Sorø, age 20, occupation entered as *Tjenende(?)* — in service, a hired servant (medium-confidence reading), heading for Springfield, Minn., under contract no. 1015 Y, with no companions listed on that contract (S83; earlier index summary S34). The register does not say why he left, and this story does not guess. Every later American census agrees that he came in 1884 (S27, S28).

He was about two months past his twentieth birthday (S31, S34). His father had died when he was three (S37). At the time he left, his mother and stepfather were already gone (S45, S46), but the 1884 sale of his stepfather's property was still months away (S50).

Registration took place in Copenhagen, about 40 miles straight-line from Bjernede (S83). The register does not name a ship, a pier, or a route (S83). Springfield, the town he named as his destination, lies about 22 miles straight-line from Redwood Falls, the county seat of Redwood County, where he would farm. Morgan, the town whose newspaper later reported his death, is about 13 miles from Springfield (S83, S145). The records do not show how he travelled from his US arrival to Redwood County.

## Marriage to Maren Sophia Jensen, 11 March 1891

Hans Peter's wife was Maren Sophia Jensen. She was born in November 1872 (S13, S27). The censuses disagree on where: the 1900 and 1910 censuses say Minnesota (S27, S28), but the 1920 census says Denmark (S29). Find a Grave also says Minnesota (S13). The records lean two to one toward Minnesota, but this story leaves the question open. Her parents were Nels Peter Jensen and Anne Marie Hansen Jensen (S13). On 11 March 1891, Hans Peter and Mary S. Jensen were married at Redwood Falls, the county seat of Redwood County, Minnesota (S272). The original marriage record survives in the Redwood County marriage book and has been viewed (S272). On the affidavit his name is written "Hans Peter Petersen"; the license on the same form spells it "Hans P. Peterson" (S272). "Mary S." is the same woman this story calls Maren Sophia; the 1900 census also lists her as "Mary" (S27, S272). Two witnesses signed: Niels Jensen and J. L. Byram (S272). The record does not say how either witness was related to the couple, if at all, and it gives no ages or parents, so this story says nothing more about them or about the ceremony itself (S272). The date fits the 1910 census, which says the couple had been married 19 years (S28).

## The farm in Brookville Township

The censuses place the family in Brookville Township, and in 1910 and 1920 they owned their farm (S27–S29, S271). Where in the township that farm stood has not been found, so this story names only the township where they farmed, not the farm itself.

**1900.** On 19 June 1900, the census taker for Brookville Township, Redwood County, listed the "Hans Peterson" household (S27). Hans was born in February 1864 in Denmark. He had come in 1884, had been in the US 16 years, and had taken the first step toward citizenship (marked "Na"). His occupation was farmer (S27). His wife was listed as "Mary," and the children were Nels A. (born October 1891), Hans O. (May 1893), Annie H. (January 1895), Louis C. (October 1896), and Mary S. (May 1898) (S27).

**1905.** The 1905 Minnesota state census found Hans P. Petersen, 41, born in 1864 in Denmark, still in Brookville Township (S273). With him were Maren S., 32, and three children: Niels A. 13, Hans R. 12, and Anna H. 10 (S273). The "Hans R." of 1905 may be the same boy as the 1900 "Hans O." and the 1910 "Rudolph H.", but that is not proven; this story gives the name exactly as the census wrote it (S27, S28, S273).

**1910.** On 23 April 1910, the family was still in Brookville Township. Hans P., 45, was a farmer who **owned his farm** (S28). Maren S., 37, born in Minnesota, was recorded as the mother of seven children, all seven living (S28). The children at home were Rudolph H. 16, Anna H. 15, Louis C. 13, Marie E. 11, Ethel M. 8, and Elmer H. 4 (S28).

**1920.** In January 1920, Hans P., 55, born in Denmark with Danish as his mother tongue, still **owned his farm** in Brookville Township, though the census marked it **mortgaged** — the farm was his, but there was still a loan on it (S29, S271). It was now a **dairy farm**, and he worked it for himself, not for wages (S29, S271). The same census says he came in 1884 and was **naturalized in 1897** — by then a citizen of his new country (S271). At home were Maren S., 47, and Anna H. 25, Ethel M. 17, Elmer H. 13, and Olga M. 8 (S29). This census gives Maren's birthplace as Denmark, but the 1900 and 1910 censuses both say Minnesota (S27, S28, S29); see above.

## The children

The children named in the records are:

- **Nels A.**, born October 1891 (S27)
- **Annie (Anna) H.**, born January 1895 (S27, S28, S29)
- **Louis C.**, born October 1896 (S27, S28)
- **Mary S.**, born May 1898 (S27). The 1910 census lists a daughter "Marie E.," 11 (S28).
- **Rudolph H. Petersen**, born 8 May 1893 in Minnesota; mother Jensen; died 13 April 1964 in Hennepin County (S95). He appears as "Rudolph H.," 16, in 1910 (S28). The 1900 census lists a son "Hans O.," born May 1893 (S27); Genealogist treats that as the same boy, because the birth month and year match Rudolph’s MNHS dates (S95). No record yet explains the change from Hans O. to Rudolph H., so the match rests on the dates.
- **Ethel Mathilda Petersen**, born 15 January 1902 in Brookville Township (S92, S152); married Werner Jensen 3 January 1923; died 20 October 1929 (S152). She appears as Ethel M., 8, in 1910 (S28).
- **Elmer Hamilton Petersen**, born 24 March 1906 in Redwood County. The Minnesota birth index registers him as "Babe" Petersen, mother Jensen (S94); later records use Elmer (S14, S15, S28). He is our line (Chapter 4).
- **Olga Margaret Petersen**, born 28 February 1911 in Redwood County; mother Jensen (S93). She appears as Olga M., 8, in 1920 (S29). Death year 1935 is still from Find a Grave only (S15).

Altogether, seven children were living in 1910 (S28), and Olga was born afterward (S93, S29), for eight children.

## Hans Peter's death

In September 1922 the *Redwood Gazette* reprinted a Morgan Messenger notice: Hans P. Petersen, age 55, of Brookville Township about two miles east of the Brookville church, died suddenly about 4:30 in the afternoon of heart trouble and complications while sitting in a chair. He had been ailing for some time; funeral arrangements were not yet made. He was survived by his wife and several children, unnamed in the notice (S145).

The records do not quite agree on his name. The newspaper calls him **Hans P.** Petersen (S145). A Minnesota death index — the FamilySearch copy of the Minnesota Historical Society index — lists a **Hans T.** Petersen who died 6 September 1922 in Redwood County, certificate 011514 (S274). Alex has noted that the "T." may be a typo in the index, but the death certificate itself has not been seen, so the difference stays open (S145, S274). This story calls him Hans P. / Hans Peter, the name Alex has chosen to use. The date 6 September 1922 also stays a candidate only; the newspaper alone does not prove the calendar day (S145, S274).

He is buried at Dannebod Cemetery in Brookville Township, Redwood County (S12). The cemetery dates from 1876 and was the burial ground of the Danish Fredsminde church, built in 1889 (S20). The wording on his stone has not yet been transcribed. The burial itself comes from Find a Grave, a secondary source, so it is held at medium confidence (S12). The cemetery is easy to find: on 180th Street just west of Skyview Avenue, near Cobden, with a historical marker on the north side of the road and the Fredsminde church monument behind it, where the church stood from 1889 to 1961 ([map](https://www.google.com/maps?q=44.31115,-94.89040), 44.31115, -94.89040).

Find a Grave places Maren Sophia’s death in 1937 and her burial at Dannebod (S13). A Minnesota Historical Society death confirmation for that year was not approved in Genealogist’s MN Pack #5, so the 1937 date and the Dannebod burial both stay secondary (Find a Grave) for now (S13).
