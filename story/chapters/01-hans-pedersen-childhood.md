# Chapter 1 — A Boy Called Hans Pedersen of Skee (1833–1847)

*This chapter follows Hans Pedersen (P18), the father of the emigrant Hans Peter Petersen. Everything here comes from Danish church books and censuses. No family memory reaches back this far.*

## Born at Valsømagle

Hans Pedersen was born on 25 February 1833 at Valsømagle, a village in Haraldsted parish, Ringsted district, Sorø county, on the island of Zealand (S51, S52, S53). He was baptized in Haraldsted church on 31 March 1833, which was Palm Sunday (S51, S52). The church stood only about 2 miles straight-line from Valsømagle, so the walk from the village to the font was a short one (S51, S53). Valsømagle is still a village today, about 4 miles from Ringsted; the church books give no farm or house for the birth, so the place to stand is the village street itself (S51, S53; [map](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/9956361203), 55.4933, 11.8213). Haraldsted Kirke still stands on Haraldstedvej in Haraldsted, 4100 Ringsted, with its churchyard — the *kirkegård* — around it ([map](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/264012020), 55.4915, 11.7682). It is the same church where he would be confirmed fourteen years later (S51, S52, S56).

The parish clerk entered him as *uægte*, meaning born outside marriage (S51, S52). His mother was an unmarried servant (*pige* / *tjenestepige*) from Valsømagle. Her name was **Ane Marie Hansdatter** (P25; Genealogist locked the patronymic 2026-11-03). The kontraministerialbog copy of the 1833 baptism writes her as **Hansd.**, set interlinear over a struck word (S52). Fourteen years later, at his confirmation, the book again calls her **Ane Marie Hansd.** (S56). The parish move lists around the birth year use the same form and trace her back and forth to Ringsted, in this order. First, Haraldsted's departure list records *Ane Marie Hansd.*, 28, *tjenestepige* (servant girl), leaving for Ringsted; the date is only a ditto mark, read as roughly November 1832 to January 1833 (S60; date LOW). Next, Ringsted's own departure list for 1833 has *Ane Marie Hansdatter*, 28, *tjenestepige*, moving to Haraldsted (S106; a likely but unverified match to her). Then, on 30 March 1833, Haraldsted's arrival list records *Ane Marie Hansd.*, 28, *tjenestepige*, come from Ringsted (S59). This was the day before her son's baptism. About a month later, around 1 May 1833, Haraldsted's departure list records *Ane Marie Hansd.*, 28, *tjenestepige*, leaving the parish again, and the destination is clearly Ringsted (S151; cleared by Alex, Decision 3, 2026-11-03, read with S59). Where she went in Ringsted is not known. No Ringsted arrival entry for her has been found, even though the 1833 arrival list was searched, May–October included (S108, S157). So the trail stops at the Haraldsted departure, and no street or house in Ringsted can be named as the place she arrived. The hovedministerialbog baptism once read as **Clausdatter** (S51); after Alex reviewed the images, that reading was set aside as superseded and Hansdatter was locked. Her parents are still unknown. A birth date of 23 March 1805 is only a CANDIDATE, not proven. Ringsted town lies roughly 4 miles straight-line from Valsømagle, so the mother's moves and the named father's home both sat within a morning's walk of the child's birthplace (S51, S59, S60).

The same baptism names a man as the child’s father. In Danish church books an *udlagt barnefader* is the man the mother named as father; the entry is not proof of paternity. The 1833 Haraldsted baptism writes **Peder Johansen** of Ringsted as that named father (S51). The 1847 confirmation repeats the same wording: *Uægte Søn af Pigen Ane Marie Hansd. og udlagt Barnefader Peder Johansen* — illegitimate son of the girl Ane Marie Hansd. and named child’s father Peder Johansen — with *pleiefader* (foster father) *Huusm.* Jens Nielsen of Skee (S56). So the record is settled on one point: the mother, Ane Marie Hansdatter, named **Peder Johansen of Ringsted** as the father, and the confirmation lists him again fourteen years later (S51, S52, S56). Genealogist has locked him as Hans’s recorded, named father (P34; cleared by Alex 2026-11-03). What the books cannot settle is biology. A named father is not a proven one, no DNA or later record shows that Peder Johansen was in fact the father, and no marriage between Ane Marie and Peder Johansen has been found. The man who raised Hans was his foster father, Jens Nielsen of Skee (S56; P26). The controversy is part of Hans’s beginning: the books name a father; the evidence does not close the case.

A year after the baptism, the census of 1834 lets us see the named father for the first time as a living man rather than a name in the margin. In Ringsted town, on the street called Sankt Hans Gade, the census lists **Peter Johansen**, about 30, *ugift* (unmarried), a *tjenestekarl* — a servant, a hired manservant — placed in the household's servants (*Tjenestefolk*) bracket (S64). Genealogist has cleared him as the same man as the Peder Johansen of Ringsted named in the baptism and the confirmation (P34; Alex, Decision B, 2026-11-03; S51, S52, S56, S64). So the father the mother named was, in 1834, an unmarried hired man of about thirty, working in service in Ringsted town, not a farmer or a householder. Sankt Hans Gade is still a street in Ringsted today, now spelled Sct. Hansgade, 4100 Ringsted, and it is the place to stand for Peter Johansen's 1834 residence ([map](https://www.openstreetmap.org/#map=18/55.4445/11.7908), 55.4445, 11.7908). The census gives no house number, so no particular house is claimed; the street itself is the visit place. That is his place in the world as the records show it. It still does not prove he was the biological father; it tells us who the named man was.

One more clue sits on the same census pages, and it is only a clue. Also listed on Sankt Hans Gade is an **Ane Marie Hansdatter**, 27, unmarried, a *tjenestepige* (maidservant), in another servants' bracket (S63). Her name and age fit Hans's mother, but she is a CANDIDATE match only, not proven to be the same woman (P25). The census does not place her in Peter Johansen's household, and no marriage between Ane Marie and Peder Johansen has been found. The street is pinned here only as Peter Johansen's 1834 residence. Because Ane Marie is only a candidate, this story does not say as fact that the couple lived on the same street, and nothing suggests they shared a house. The May 1833 departure to Ringsted (S151) also does not prove where the mother lived in town; the two records are separate.

## The world he was born into

The records tell us where Hans was born; the encyclopedias tell us what that country looked like. Valsømagle and Haraldsted lay in Sorø county (*Sorø Amt*): in 1798 the old Ringsted county had been merged into it, and only a few years after Hans's birth, in 1837 and 1841, the parishes and market towns were made into local municipalities of their own (K1)(K2). Ringsted, about 4 miles from Valsømagle, was the market town for the parishes around it (K2).

Haraldsted itself was an old place even then. In the Middle Ages it had been a royal estate and a pilgrimage village, because Duke Knud Lavard was murdered in the nearby forest in 1131 (K4)(K5). The parsonage that still stands southeast of the church — the *præstegård* named in Chapter 1's 1797 betrothal — is a thatched, half-timbered four-wing house rebuilt after a fire in 1789, so it was barely forty years old when Hans was baptized (K6; S222). The sources do not say that Hans or his mother ever went inside it.

The countryside had been remade within living memory. The villages of the Ringsted district were enclosed — the old shared fields split into separate holdings — in roughly 1780–1810; some farms stayed in the village and others moved out onto their own land (K1). Farms dominated this district, and cottagers (*husmænd*) were few compared with farmers (K1)(K11). Those are district-wide statements; how many farms and houses Valsømagle or Skee had in 1833 is not in the sources used here.

Denmark in 1833 was still an absolute monarchy: the king ruled without a parliament, and would until the June Constitution of 1849 (K15). The old *stavnsbånd*, which had tied peasant men to the estate where they were born, had been abolished in 1788, some forty-five years before Hans was born (K18). The 1814 school ordinances had made schooling compulsory for ordinary children (K20); which school served Skee or Valsømagle, and whether Hans attended it, is UNVERIFIED and not found in the records.

## A foster son in Skee

Hans did not grow up with his mother. On the census of 18 February 1834, when he was one year old, he was living in the village of Skee in Haraldsted parish, about 3 miles straight-line from Valsømagle where he was born and about 3 miles from Haraldsted church. He was listed as a *pleiebarn* (foster child) in the household of Jens Nielsen, a cottager (*husmand*) and wheelwright, and his wife Kirsten Johansdatter (S54).

He was still there at the next two censuses:

- **1 February 1840:** "Hans Pedersen, 6, unmarried, foster child" in the household of Jens Nielsen, 49, and Kirsten, 44 (S55).
- **1 February 1845:** "Hans Pedersen, 12," born in the parish, now called a *pleiesøn* (foster son) (S57).

So from his first year until at least age twelve, the cottage of Jens and Kirsten in Skee was his home (S54, S55, S57). No record found so far shows that the foster parents were related to him or to his mother.

The records show that Jens Nielsen worked as a wheelwright (S54). Many years later, Hans himself would be a wheelwright, a *hjulmand* (S36, S37). The sources do not say who taught him the trade. We can only note that he grew up in a wheelwright's home, and that by 1850 Jens Nielsen was still entered as *huusmand* and, in a hard-to-read word, perhaps *hjulmager* (S58). The thread of the trade runs unbroken from the foster father's cottage to Hans's own marriage entry fifteen years later (S36).

## Confirmation, 1847

On 11 April 1847, the first Sunday after Easter, Hans was confirmed in Haraldsted church. Confirmation was not optional: it had been compulsory in Denmark since 1736, and it required the young person to pass a test of what they had learned (K19). He was entered as boy no. 3: "Hans Pedersen of Skee," born 25 February 1833 at Valsømagle and vaccinated 29 June 1833 by Aschlund (S56). The full confirmation line is the richest single sentence we have for his childhood: *Hans Pedersen af Skee … Uægte Søn af Pigen Ane Marie Hansd. og udlagt Barnefader Peder Johansen — Pleiefader Huusm. Jens Nielsen i Skee* (S56). In one entry the parish records his foster home in Skee, his mother as Hansd., and the named father from Ringsted, whose biological fatherhood is still unproven. Folio 41, no. 1026, is the book’s own reference for that confirmation (S56). A visitor today can walk the churchyard that surrounds Haraldsted church — the ground he crossed on his baptism day and again at fourteen (S51, S56).

## A betrothal at the parsonage, 1797 (CANDIDATE)

One older record may reach back toward Ane Marie's own family, but it is not proven. On 21 May 1797, at Haraldsted Præstegaarden — the parish parsonage — the widower (*enkemand*) Hans Pedersen and the girl (*pige*) Maren Hansdatter, written *Maren Hans D.*, both of Walsøemagle (Valsømagle), were betrothed, *trolovede* (S222). They were married on 15 October 1797 (S222). The betrothal and the place are clear in the record. Whether this couple were Ane Marie's parents is **Provisional — a CANDIDATE only**: a daughter named Hansdatter from the same village would fit, but her own baptism has not been found, so nothing ties her to them yet (S222; P25). Even the bride's name leans Maren rather than Marie, but that too is still being checked.

The parsonage still stands about 80 metres southeast of Haraldsted church, at Haraldstedvej 91 ([map](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/341232574), 55.4910, 11.7692). It is a working private home and parish office today, so the place to stand is the road or the churchyard side, not the grounds. The record does not say in which room the couple stood, and this story does not guess.

## What we do not know yet

- Whether **Peder Johansen** of Ringsted was Hans’s biological father remains open. He is locked as the recorded, *named* father (P34; S51, S52, S56) and, by Decision B, as the 1834 Sankt Hans Gade *tjenestekarl* (S64); biological paternity is not proven, and no marriage to Ane Marie has been found.
- Where Ane Marie Hansdatter went in Ringsted after she left Haraldsted around 1 May 1833 is still open. No Ringsted arrival entry has been found (S151; S108, S157 negative).
- Who Ane Marie Hansdatter’s parents were, and when exactly she was born, is still open. Her patronymic is locked as Hansdatter (P25), but 23 March 1805 is only a CANDIDATE birth date. A Valsømagle couple, Hans Pedersen and Maren Hansdatter, betrothed at the Haraldsted parsonage in 1797 (S222), are a CANDIDATE pair for her parents only — her baptism is missing, and they are not locked as her parents.
- Whether the Ane Marie Hansdatter, 27, *tjenestepige*, on Sankt Hans Gade in 1834 (S63) is Hans's mother is not proven; she is a CANDIDATE match for P25 only, and the census does not place her in Peter Johansen's household.
- At the census of 1 February 1850 Hans, then about 17, was no longer in the Skee foster household (S58). Where he was living that year has not been found.
- The years between 1850 and 1862 are blank in our records. The next time we see him, he is a young man working at Bjernedegaard in Bjernede parish (Chapter 2).
