Chapter 2 — Bjernede: A Marriage, Four Children, and Loss (1862–1884)

This chapter covers Hans Pedersen (P18) and Ane Rasmusdatter (P19), their sons, and the second family Ane started after she was widowed. The facts come from the Bjernede church books, Danish censuses, and a 1884 property deed. These people are documented in records, but the family did not hand down their names. Alex has confirmed the birthplace (Bjernede) and the birth year (1864) of Hans Peter (S30).

The wedding, 14 October 1862

On 14 October 1862, Hans Pedersen married Ane Rasmusdatter in Bjernede church in Sorø county (S36). The register calls him a bachelor (ungkarl) and a wheelwright (hjulmand), born 25 February 1833 at a place the clerk wrote as something like "Walsmagle" — Valsømagle, about 8 miles straight-line from Bjernede (S36, S51).Map · ValsømagleGoogle It calls her a maiden (pige), born 17 June 1843 (S36). Her birthplace was entered as what may read "Bjernede Lodsgaard," but that reading is uncertain (S36). Her parents have not yet been found. Bjernede church, where they married, lies about 6 miles straight-line from Haraldsted church, where Hans had been baptized and confirmed (S36, S56).Map · Haraldsted KirkeGoogle Bjernede Kirke still stands at Bjernede Kirkevej 1A, 4180 Sorø — Zealand's only surviving round church, about 5 km (3 miles) northeast of Sorø, built probably in the 1160s–70s by Sune Ebbesen of the Hvide family (K8)(K9; map pin from Places Scout) (, 55.4621, 11.6251).Map · Bjernede KirkeGoogle The same building saw this wedding, Hans Peter's baptism and confirmation, and the burials of his baby brother and his father (S31, S32, S36, S37, S41).

The Denmark of their wedding

Hans and Ane married in a different Denmark from the one Hans had been born into. Under the June Constitution of 5 June 1849 the king gave up absolute power and a parliament, the Rigsdag, was created. Men over 30 with their own household could vote, except people convicted of crimes and people on poor relief (K15). The records do not say whether Hans ever voted.

The railway had come, too. In 1856 the western line from Roskilde was extended to Korsør, and Ringsted, Fjenneslev, and Sorø all got stations that year — about six years before the wedding and about ten before Hans Peter's birth (K2)(K22). Nothing in the records says the family ever rode it.

Many houses in the Danish countryside had been built during the enclosure years and often stood together in cottager colonies (husmandskolonier) at the edge of the village fields; few new ones were built after 1810 (K11)(K24). The family's place name, Bjernedegaardshuse — "the Bjernedegaard houses" — sounds like such a cluster, but whether it was one is UNVERIFIED. What the records do say is Hans's trade: he was a wheelwright, hjulmand, at his wedding, at Rasmus's baptism, and at his burial (S36, S43, S37).

A first son who lived one day

Their first child was a boy, born 17 January 1863. The register labels him "1st child" (S42). He died the next day, 18 January, and the entry seems to say he died unbaptized, so he never received a name (S42).

Hans Peter, 1864

Their second son was born on 28 February 1864 and baptized in Bjernede on 5 June 1864 as Hans Peder Pedersen, son of Hans Pedersen and Ane Rasmusdatter (S31). He is the man who later became Hans Peter Petersen of Redwood County, Minnesota. Alex has confirmed that this is "our Hans" (S30).

He was born into a war year. Denmark's war with Prussia and Austria over Schleswig began around the start of February 1864 (exact start date UNVERIFIED in the sources used here) (K23). On 18 April 1864 the Dybbøl position was stormed — more than 35,000 Prussian troops against about 10,000 Danes — and the Danes fell back to Als (K23). By the Peace of Vienna, later that year (treaty date UNVERIFIED here), Denmark gave up the duchies (K23). So the whole war ran through Hans Peter's first months, between his birth on 28 February and well after his baptism on 5 June (S31, K23). No record says that his father or any man of Bjernede served, and how the war was felt in Sorø county is not known from the sources used here.

Rasmus, winter 1866–67

A third son, Rasmus Pedersen, was born on 10 December 1866 at Bjernedegaardshuse and baptized in church on 23 January 1867. The register gives his father's trade as wheelwright (S43). Rasmus died on 5 February 1867 and was buried on 10 February (S41, S43). The dates put him at about eight weeks old, but the burial entry's age column seems to read "12 Dage" (12 days), a reading marked uncertain (S41). The two books do not agree, and this story leaves the discrepancy open.

Hans Pedersen's death, September 1867

Seven months later, the father died too. Hans Pedersen, wheelwright of Bjernedegaardshuse, died on 11 September 1867 and was buried at Bjernede on 17 September. He was 34 (S37). The burial entry calls him "Søn af Ane Marie Hans Dat.(?)", another Hansdatter-style reading of his mother's name (S37; see Chapter 1). Hans Peter was three and a half when his father was laid in Bjernede churchyard (S31, S37).Map · Bjernede KirkeGoogle Baby Rasmus had been buried in this churchyard seven months earlier (S41). No grave from 1867 is known to survive; the family can stand in the churchyard where both were buried, not at a marked grave. The cause of death in the register is too hard to read, so this chapter leaves it out.

Bjernedegaardshuse, Bjernedehuse, Bjernede

The books name the family's places in three overlapping ways: Bjernedegaardshuse (the houses belonging to Bjernedegaard) for Rasmus's birth and his father's death in 1866–67 (S37, S43), Bjernedehuse for the half-siblings' births in 1871 and 1874 (S39, S47, S48), and plain Bjernede in the censuses (S44, S33). All of it lies within about a mile of Bjernede church.Map · Bjernede KirkeGoogle Which house the family lived in has not been proven. A visitor can walk among the houses of Bjernede, for example near the Stokholtsvej area about a kilometre east-southeast of the church, but no single house can be named as theirs.

A widow and her boy, 1870

On the census of 1 February 1870, Ane appears as "Anne Rasmusen," 27, a widow. She is living in a house in Bjernede with her son "Hans Peter Pedersen, 6" (S44).

A second marriage and a second family

In spring 1870, Ane married Hans Rasmussen in Bjernede. The day of the wedding cannot be read (S38). He was a widower marrying for the third time, and this was her second marriage (S38). The death record of Hans Rasmussen gives his age as 83 in 1883 (S46), so he was much older than Ane. His birth year is given differently in different records (S38, S46).

Hans Peter gained two half-siblings:

  • Rasmus Peter Rasmussen, born 1 March 1871 at Bjernedehuse and baptized 4 April 1871 (S39, S47).
  • Ane Marie Rasmussen, born 24 October 1874 at Bjernedehuse. Her baptism is recorded as 10 January 1875 in one book (S40) and 11 January 1875 in another (S48).

Hans Peter's confirmation and the 1880 census

Hans Peter was confirmed in Bjernede on Palm Sunday, 14 April 1878. His parents were entered as boelsmand (smallholder) Hans Petersen and Ane Rasmusdatter, and his birth date as 28 February 1864 (S32).

On the census of 1 February 1880, a "Hans Peder Pedersen, 15," appears as a stepson in the household of Hans Rasmussen and Ane Rasmussen in Bjernede (S33). Parts of this entry are hard to read.

1883: the household ends

Hans Rasmussen, a smallholder (bolmand) of Bjernede Huse, died in early July 1883, about the 2nd, and was buried a few days later. The exact days are hard to read (S46). His father is named as Rasmus Hansen (S46).

Three days or so earlier, on 29 June 1883, an "Ane Marie Rasmussen," age 40 and the wife in her second marriage of smallholder Hans Rasmussen of Bjerne Huse, died. She was buried on 5 July 1883 (S45). Provisional: she is very likely Ane Rasmusdatter, because the husband, age, and second marriage all match. But the entry calls her "Ane Marie" and does not name her parents, so the identification is an inference (S45). What is certain is that the confirmation records of both younger children say that both parents were dead (S47, S48).

The estate of matr. 5a, 1884

Hans Rasmussen had owned a property in Bjernede parish recorded as matr. no. 5 A af Bjernedehuse (S50). On 13 November 1884, his estate and that of his late wife sold it to J.P. Mathiesen, a miller of Gyrstinge, for 13,000 kroner (S50). Gyrstinge lies about 3 miles straight-line from Bjernede. It was one of the bigger villages of the Ringsted district, with 24 farms, and unlike most of its neighbours it had belonged to Sorø Klosters Len after 1536 (K1). The deed also required the buyer to honor an existing lease to Indsidder (lodger) Hans Andersen, running from 12 December 1867 (S50). The deed describes the property as having "hitherto belonged" to Hans Rasmussen (S50). Hans Pedersen did not own it (S50), and whether young Hans Peter ever lived on 5a is not proven.Map · Matr. 5aGoogleParcel pin. Stand near Stokholtsvej 35. The buildings are on parcel 4 and are not the 1800s house. Living on 5a is not proven. A mortgage bond of 3,600 kroner was recorded as owed to "the minors Rasmus Peter and Ane Marie Rasmussen," children of the late Hans Rasmussen and his wife (S50).

The orphaned half-siblings went to foster homes:

  • At his spring 1885 confirmation, Rasmus Peter's foster father was farmer Rasmus Rasmussen, of a place read as Fuldby (S47).
  • At her autumn 1888 confirmation, Ane Marie's foster father was smallholder Anders Larsen of Bjernedehuse (S48).

Hans Peter was 20 by the time of the sale and does not appear on the deed (S50). By then he had already left Denmark (Chapter 3, S83).

What we do not know yet

  • Ane Rasmusdatter's parents and her baptism.
  • The cause of Hans Pedersen's death.
  • Rasmus's age at death: about eight weeks by the dates, "12 Dage"(?) in the burial entry (S41, S43).
  • Whether Ane Marie Rasmussen of 1883 is Ane Rasmusdatter (Provisional, S45, S50).
  • Which house in Bjernedehuse was Hans Peter's childhood home.
  • Who Hans Rasmussen's first two wives were.
  • What happened to Ane Marie Rasmussen after 1888.
